# Tone of Voice — Timmerfabriek Sint Nyk

## Core character
Timmerfabriek Sint Nyk is a small-scale local company. The writing should feel like speaking with a capable, approachable craft business: knowledgeable about the work, easy to reach and straightforward about what it can do.

The brand should feel:
- nuchter;
- vakbekwaam;
- persoonlijk;
- betrouwbaar;
- praktisch;
- lokaal geworteld;
- zorgvuldig, without sounding precious or luxurious.

## The desired impression
After reading a page, a potential customer should think:

> Deze mensen maken dit zelf, weten waar ze over praten en ik kan ze gewoon bellen om mijn situatie te bespreken.

Not:

> Dit klinkt als een marketingbureau dat zoveel mogelijk superlatieven gebruikt.

## Voice
### We are
- A small local timber workshop, not a faceless national chain.
- Specialists who explain things in understandable Dutch.
- Helpful and matter-of-fact.
- Confident where facts support confidence.
- Specific about products, choices and process.

### We are not
- loud;
- corporate;
- overly polished;
- salesy;
- boastful;
- full of clichés;
- artificially folksy or stereotypically Frisian.

## Addressing the reader
Use **u/uw** consistently in customer-facing website copy unless the existing brand direction is deliberately changed later.

Use **we/wij** for the company. Prefer `we` in normal sentences; use `wij` only where natural emphasis is useful.

## Sentence style
- Prefer short to medium-length sentences.
- Put the useful information early in the sentence.
- Use active constructions.
- One idea per sentence where possible.
- Paragraphs should usually be short enough to scan comfortably on mobile.
- Technical terminology is welcome when it helps the customer, but explain unfamiliar terms.

## Punctuation
Never use an em dash (`—`) or en dash (`–`) in Dutch website copy. It reads as English or AI-generated and does not fit this site's voice.

This applies to every piece of human-facing Dutch text, not only visible body copy: titles, meta descriptions, CTAs, navigation labels, `aria-label`s, `alt` text, and other accessibility/UI copy all follow the same rule. It does not apply to code-technical uses of a similar character that aren't human copy.

Depending on the sentence, replace it with:
- a comma;
- a period, splitting the sentence in two;
- a colon;
- parentheses;
- or a natural rewrite.

A regular hyphen (`-`) stays allowed where it belongs grammatically, for example in compound words or fixed notations.

## Specific beats generic
Prefer concrete statements.

**Prefer:**
> We maken houten kozijnen op maat in onze eigen werkplaats in Sint Nicolaasga.

**Avoid:**
> Wij bieden hoogwaardige maatwerkoplossingen die volledig aansluiten op uw unieke wensen.

**Prefer:**
> Heeft u al tekeningen of maatvoering? Stuur ze gerust mee met uw aanvraag.

**Avoid:**
> Neem vandaag nog contact op en ontdek de ongekende mogelijkheden.

## Marketing clichés to avoid
Avoid these unless there is a strong factual reason to use them:
- dé specialist;
- toonaangevend;
- hoogwaardige totaaloplossingen;
- unieke oplossingen;
- passie voor ons vak;
- ontzorgen;
- ongekende mogelijkheden;
- kwaliteit staat bij ons voorop;
- voor ieder wat wils;
- van A tot Z;
- jarenlange ervaring (unless exact and verified);
- scherp geprijsd / beste prijs;
- snel geleverd (unless supported by a concrete promise).

Words such as `vakmanschap`, `maatwerk`, `kwaliteit` and `persoonlijk` may be used, but should usually be demonstrated with concrete information instead of repeated as empty claims.

## Local character
Local relevance should arise naturally from real facts:
- the workshop is in Sint Nicolaasga;
- Friesland can be mentioned where relevant;
- project locations can be mentioned when verified;
- nearby areas may be referenced when genuinely served.

Do not force town names into paragraphs for SEO. Do not pretend the business works only locally if its verified service area is wider.

## Expertise
Show expertise by answering practical questions, for example:
- what choices a customer has;
- what information is needed for a quotation;
- how measuring or preparation works;
- differences between product types;
- what matters for renovation versus new build;
- glazing or finishing choices when verified.

Do not prove expertise by repeatedly calling the company an expert.

## Calls to action
CTAs should be low-pressure and practical.

Good direction:
- `Vraag een offerte aan`
- `Bespreek uw project`
- `Neem contact op`
- `Stuur uw tekeningen of maten mee`
- `Heeft u een vraag over de uitvoering? Neem gerust contact op.`

Avoid false urgency such as:
- `Wacht niet langer!`
- `Profiteer nu!`
- `Vraag vandaag nog uw droomkozijnen aan!`

## B2C versus contractor copy
### Private customers
Explain choices and process clearly. Do not assume construction knowledge. Focus on the situation in the home/project and make the next step easy.

### Contractors/professionals
Be more concise and technical where useful. Focus on drawings, dimensions, specifications, coordination, production, planning and delivery — but only make claims that are verified.

The underlying voice remains the same for both audiences.

## SEO language
Keywords must fit normal Dutch. Never write an awkward phrase purely because it appears in keyword research.

Do not repeat `Timmerfabriek Sint Nyk`, `Friesland`, `Sint Nicolaasga`, or the primary keyword in every paragraph.

Use synonyms and closely related concepts naturally when they help explain the subject.

## AI-writing warning signs
Rewrite copy when it contains:
- three or more adjectives where one concrete fact would work;
- repetitive conclusions at the end of every section;
- repeated `Of u nu ... of ...` constructions;
- repeated `Niet alleen ..., maar ook ...` constructions;
- unnecessary rhetorical questions;
- generic opening paragraphs that could belong to any competitor;
- a heading followed by a paragraph that merely repeats the heading;
- claims with no supporting detail.

## Final tone test
Before approving copy, ask:
1. Could a local craft business naturally say this to a customer?
2. Is it concrete?
3. Is it understandable without specialist knowledge?
4. Does it sound calm rather than salesy?
5. Is every company claim supported by known facts?
6. Could this paragraph be pasted onto any competitor's site unchanged? If yes, make it more specific or remove it.
